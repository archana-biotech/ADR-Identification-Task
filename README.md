# ADR Identification Basics – Task 1

## Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) Identification

### 1. Introduction

An Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is an unwanted or harmful response associated with the use of a medicinal product.

ADR identification involves reviewing the patient's symptoms, medication history, and the timeline between starting a medicine and developing symptoms. This helps identify a suspected drug and assess whether the reaction may be drug-related.

### Suspected Drug: Amoxicillin

Amoxicillin is an antibiotic used to treat various bacterial infections.

The appearance of **rash and itching** after starting amoxicillin may indicate a hypersensitivity or allergic reaction. Nausea and dizziness can also occur as adverse effects with medicines.

The temporal relationship between starting the medicine and the appearance of symptoms supports amoxicillin as a **suspected drug**.

## 6. Possible Type of Reaction

The presence of **rash and itching** may be consistent with a hypersensitivity/allergic reaction.

Drug-related allergic reactions can involve the immune system and may range from mild skin reactions to serious systemic reactions.

The exact type and severity of the reaction require clinical assessment.

---

## 7. Causality Assessment

Causality assessment is used to determine whether a medicine may have caused an adverse reaction.

Important factors include:

1. Temporal relationship between drug exposure and reaction
2. Known association between the drug and the reaction
3. Improvement after withdrawal, when applicable
4. Recurrence after re-exposure, when appropriate
5. Other possible causes
6. Relevant medical and medication history

Common causality assessment methods include:

- **WHO-UMC causality assessment**
- **Naranjo algorithm**

In this case, the available information suggests a **suspected relationship**, but additional clinical information would be required for a definitive causality assessment.

---

## 8. Pharmacovigilance

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities concerned with the detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse effects or other medicine-related problems.

The major objectives of pharmacovigilance include:

- Detecting adverse drug reactions
- Assessing the safety of medicines
- Understanding medicine-related risks
- Preventing avoidable harm
- Improving patient safety
- Maintaining a favorable benefit-risk balance

---

## 9. ADR Reporting

Important information in an ADR report may include:

### Patient information
- Age
- Sex
- Weight
- Patient initials or other appropriate identification

### Suspected reaction
- Description of symptoms
- Date of onset
- Outcome
- Seriousness of the reaction

### Suspected medicine
- Generic/brand name
- Dose
- Route
- Frequency
- Start and stop dates
- Indication for use

### Other information
- Concomitant medicines
- Relevant medical history
- Laboratory findings
- Action taken with the suspected medicine
- Outcome of the reaction

Patient confidentiality should always be maintained while reporting an ADR.
